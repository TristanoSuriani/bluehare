# Design principles

## Target user
The target user is a musician or producer who wants to make music in a simple and enjoyable way. They may want to just turn
on the device and make beautiful sounds, or they may want to explore the details of the music and the device. They may be beginners or experienced musicians, but they are not necessarily programmers or sound designers.

## Turn on and play
The user must be able to make music as soon as they turn on the device. A curated list of presets and styles should be available, and the user should be able to select them with the minimum amount of actions.

## Don't make me think
The interface should be intuitive and straightforward, allowing users to focus on creating music rather than figuring out how to use the device.

## Ease of sound and music creation goes before feature completeness
The device should prioritize ease of use and the ability to create music over having a large number of features. The goal is to make music creation accessible and enjoyable, rather than overwhelming the user with options.

## User exploration is encouraged
The device should encourage users to explore and experiment with different sounds and musical ideas. It should provide a safe and supportive environment for users to learn and grow as musicians, allowing them to discover new possibilities and develop their own unique style.

## UI must be consistent and predictable and easy to read also for people with impaired vision
The user interface should be consistent and predictable, making it easy for users to understand how to interact with the device. It should also be designed with accessibility in mind, ensuring that users with impaired vision can easily read and navigate the interface. This may include using high-contrast colors, clear typography, and providing alternative text for visual elements. Baud Girl's excellent firmware's UI  can be used as a source of inspiration.

## Every action must be reversible
The device should allow users to easily undo and redo actions, providing a safety net for experimentation and exploration. This will help users feel more confident in trying new things and making changes to their music without fear of losing their work.

## Big picture first, details later
The device should present the user with a clear overview of their music and the current state of the device, allowing them to understand the big picture before diving into the details. This will help users make informed decisions about their music and the device, and will encourage them to explore and experiment with different ideas.
